1. Numerical Feature Distributions
----------------------------------
Histograms of numerical features show the overall distribution and skewness of each variable.
Several features exhibit right-skewed distributions, indicating the presence of high-value outliers.
Boxplots further confirm the existence of outliers, especially in price-related features.

2. Categorical Feature Analysis
-------------------------------
Bar charts of categorical variables (top 20 categories) reveal that some features have dominant
categories, while others show a long-tail distribution. This suggests that rare categories may need
to be grouped or encoded carefully during feature engineering.

3. Price Distribution
---------------------
The price distribution is right-skewed, indicating that most laptops fall into lower to mid-price
ranges, with fewer high-end expensive models. A log transformation of price may be beneficial for
modeling.

4. Price by Company
-------------------
The boxplot of Price by Company (Top 10 brands) shows clear price differences among manufacturers.
Premium brands tend to have higher median prices and wider price ranges, while budget brands show
lower and more compact distributions.

5. Numerical Features vs Price
-------------------------------
Scatter plots between numerical features and price suggest varying degrees of correlation.
Some features display a positive relationship with price, while others show weak or no clear trends.
These plots help identify potentially important predictors for regression models.

6. Correlation Heatmap
---------------------
The correlation heatmap visualizes linear relationships among numerical features.
Strong correlations between certain variables indicate possible redundancy, while features with
higher correlation to price are likely to be more informative for predictive modeling.

Overall, this EDA provides insights into data quality, feature distributions, and relationships,
forming a solid foundation for data cleaning, feature engineering, and model selection.
